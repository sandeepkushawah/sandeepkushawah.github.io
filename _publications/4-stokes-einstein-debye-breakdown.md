**Abstract.** The breakdown of Stokes–Einstein–Debye (SED) relations in supercooled glass-forming liquids is primarily driven by two distinct mechanisms: translational–rotational decoupling and the failure of the Debye rotational diffusion model. Disentangling these mechanisms is crucial for a complete microscopic understanding of rotational dynamics near the glass transition. To address this, we present a molecular dynamics study of a binary mixture of dumbbell molecules bonded via the finitely extensible nonlinear elastic (FENE) potential and interacting through a standard glass-forming Lennard-Jones potential. This system provides a simplified framework for investigating rotational dynamics in a phase-separated geometry. We specifically examine how these two mechanisms manifest in a system containing a dense liquid slab coexisting with a dilute vapour phase, generated by cavitation at a reduced number density of ρ = 0.8. This heterogeneous environment allows us to distinguish the specific contributions of Debye model breakdown and translational–rotational decoupling to the violation of SED relations. Our key finding is that these two mechanisms exhibit opposite density dependences: the Debye model breaks down more strongly at higher density (ρ = 1.2), while translational–rotational decoupling is more pronounced at lower density (ρ = 0.8), where cavitation introduces structural and dynamical inhomogeneity.

**Keywords:** Stokes–Einstein–Debye relation, rotational dynamics, translational–rotational decoupling, supercooled liquids, FENE dumbbells, cavitation, molecular dynamics.
