Overview

As a glass-forming liquid is cooled toward the glass transition, its relaxation slows by many orders of magnitude — and the slowing is spatially non-uniform. Some particles hop out of their cages while their neighbours stay nearly frozen. This project asks how the organisation of that heterogeneity changes when the liquid is compressed: the Kob–Andersen 80:20 mixture is studied at four densities (ρ = 1.2, 1.4, 1.6, 1.8), each at its own mode-coupling temperature Tc, so every liquid sits at the same point of the same dynamical crossover.

Method

  • Freeze the clock at t*, the peak of the non-Gaussian parameter α2(t) — the time of maximal heterogeneity.
  • Rank every A particle by its maximum displacement in [0, t*]; the top 5% are mobile, the bottom 5% immobile.
  • Measure their mobility-resolved pair correlations, cluster-size and string-length distributions, fractal dimension, relaxation times and label memory.

Fast and slow particles occupy separate territories at every density — mobile (red) and immobile (blue) 5% at each density's Tc.

Key findings

  • Universal architecture. In reduced units, the shape of the mobility distribution, the non-Gaussian hopping tail, mobility-resolved g(r), the cluster-size exponent and the fractal dimension of mobile clusters are essentially the same at all four densities.
  • A percolated mobile network. At Tc the largest mobile cluster contains most of the mobile particles at every density; strings — quasi-linear chains in which each particle replaces the one ahead — provide its internal connectivity.
  • Density-dependent contrast in time. What changes under compression is the contrast between the populations: mobile particles lose part of their speed advantage, and the relative durability of the mobile and immobile labels inverts.

The longest mobile string at each density: green at t = 0, red at t = t*. Displacements point along the chain.
