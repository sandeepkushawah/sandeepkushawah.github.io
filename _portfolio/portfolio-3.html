Overview

In a dense supercooled liquid the first peak of g(r) changes only slightly on cooling, while the relaxation time τα grows enormously. This project asks whether that small structural change nevertheless carries the mean relaxation time — and why the Kob–Andersen (KA) mixture and its purely repulsive twin (KAWCA), nearly identical in structure, relax so differently at low density yet almost identically under compression.

Approach

  • Model how density relaxes inside a crowded cage: a particle can move only by displacing its neighbours, along quasi-one-dimensional channels between them.
  • Take the free volume along those channels, which lie on the shell of the most probable neighbour distance, and read it from the height of the first peak of g(r).
  • Test the resulting free-volume law on KA and KAWCA at densities ρ = 1.2–1.8.

(a) A 3D KA cage at ρ = 1.2. (b) Schematic of relaxation channels: preferred path (black), blocked path (red), string moves (green).

Key findings

  • Density relaxation is dominated by short, nearly one-dimensional pair moves along the channels set by the cage.
  • With the first-shell occupancy fixed, the first-peak height of g(r) acts as a free-volume coordinate that tracks τα in both models and at every density.
  • The Vogel–Fulcher–Tammann divergence maps onto jamming of the relaxation channel; attraction acts through the channel width, which is why KA and KAWCA differ most at low density.

Status
